# Humanizer 2.0: The Structural Transformation Playbook

This playbook provides actionable rewrite recipes to transform synthetic AI drafts into structurally robust human narrative prose. It targets the 30 discriminative features identified in *StoryScope* (COLM 2026).

---

## Transformation 1: De-Synthesizing the Narrative (The Moralectomy)

### The AI Pattern
AI models cannot resist summarizing what a story "means". They produce closing paragraphs that act like moral fables or self-help epiphanies.

### The Rewrite Recipe
1. **The Scissors Rule**: Inspect the final 3 paragraphs of the story. If any paragraph contains the phrases *"realized that"*, *"in that moment, he knew"*, *"it was not about X, but about Y"*, or *"the true meaning of"*, **delete it completely**.
2. **The Tangible Cut**: End the story 1–2 beats earlier on a physical action, an interrupted utterance, or an external environmental fact.
3. **Contrast Example**:

```text
[BEFORE - AI MORALIZING]
As Elena locked the clinic doors, she looked out at the rain-soaked streets. She finally 
realized that healing was not about erasing grief, but learning to walk alongside it. 
Her patients had not only survived; they had taught her what it truly meant to be whole.

[AFTER - HUMANIZER 2.0]
Elena locked the deadbolt and rattled the brass handle twice to make sure. Her left heel 
was rubbing raw against the leather of her shoe. Across the street, the lights in the 
twenty-four-hour laundromat flickered, buzzed, and stayed on. She pulled her collar up 
and started walking toward the subway.
```

## Transformation 2: Somatic De-Embodiment (Cooling the Flesh)

### The AI Pattern
AI writes about characters as if they are autonomic nervous systems with a keyboard attached. Every sentence is packed with throat-tightening, chest-pounding, bile-rising, and breath-catching.

### The Rewrite Recipe
1. **Direct Emotion Swap**: Replace somatic reflex metaphors with cold, direct emotional labels or situational understatements.
2. **Purge the Olfactory**: Unless the smell is a primary plot clue (e.g., gas leak in a detective story), delete words like *ozone, copper, rot, petrichor, damp cellar*.
3. **Contrast Example**:

```text
[BEFORE - AI EMBODIED SLOP]
A chill rippled down Julian's spine. His throat constricted, and a knot of dread tightened 
in the pit of his stomach. His heart hammered violently against his ribcage as the scent 
of copper and burning ozone filled the cramped chamber, choking the breath in his lungs.

[AFTER - HUMANIZER 2.0]
Julian was deeply unsettled. The transformer unit behind the partition was making a high-pitched 
whine that hadn't been there on Thursday, and the ventilation fan had died again. He checked 
his watch: 5:45 PM. He really did not want to stay late on a Friday.
```

## Transformation 3: Temporal Disruption (Breaking the Conveyor Belt)

### The AI Pattern
AI tells stories in strict chronological order: Event A causes Event B, which causes Event C, marching obediently toward the climax without anachrony or narrative jumps.

### The Rewrite Recipe
1. **Asynchronous Entry**: Begin the scene 20 minutes *after* the dramatic crisis occurred, showing characters dealing with the awkward bureaucratic, domestic, or physical aftermath.
2. **Flash-Forwards & Distant Recontextualization**: Use Genette's prolepsis (flash-forwards) or ellipses (time jumps). Jump 8 months ahead, then look back with incomplete recall.
3. **Contrast Example**:

```text
[BEFORE - AI LINEAR ARC]
1. David receives the eviction notice.
2. David feels a wave of panic.
3. David goes to the landlord's office to plead.
4. Landlord refuses.
5. David returns home and resolves to fight back.

[AFTER - HUMANIZER 2.0]
Open: Six weeks after the eviction, David is living in a motel room, searching for his mislaid 
tax returns under a bed frame.
Flashback (Anachrony): The memory of the landlord's secretary sipping a diet soda while handing 
him the pink envelope.
Delayed Disclosure: David reveals he didn't even read the notice until three days later because 
he had been preoccupied with his car's failing alternator.
```

## Transformation 4: Injecting Intertextuality & Cultural Density

### The AI Pattern
AI lives in a generic, sterile universe. It avoids referencing specific published authors, historical events, brand names, or real cultural artifacts, preferring vague, generic tropes (*"an ancient tome"*, *"a famous philosopher"*).

### The Rewrite Recipe
1. **Specific Cultural Grounding**: Name real, specific historical figures, books, songs, or institutions rather than generic stand-ins.
2. **Contrast Example**:

```text
[BEFORE - AI VAGUE ALLUSION]
He pulled a dusty volume of classic poetry from the wooden shelf, searching for words 
that might bring comfort to his troubled mind. An old philosopher once said that patience 
is the root of wisdom.

[AFTER - HUMANIZER 2.0]
He pulled down a battered 1994 paperback of Philip Larkin's *Collected Poems* that he had 
stolen from a community college library in Rochester. On page 42, someone had underlined 
two lines in purple ballpoint and written "BS" in the margin.
```

## Transformation 5: Subplot Friction & Moral Ambiguity

### The AI Pattern
AI eliminates subplots (79% have none). The protagonist is clearly virtuous, their conflict is singular, and every conversation directly serves the main problem.

### The Rewrite Recipe
1. **Introduce an Unrelated Secondary Problem (B-Plot)**: Give the character an irritating, low-stakes logistical distraction that constantly competes with the main plot for their attention.
2. **Permit Petty / Ambivalent Behavior**: Let the protagonist act out of annoyance, vanity, exhaustion, or greed without framing them as an evil villain.
3. **Contrast Example**:

```text
[BEFORE - AI HEROIC CONVERGENCE]
Sarah stood before the town council, resolute and passionate. She presented the evidence 
of the chemical leak, her moral clarity inspiring the council members to reconsider their 
silence.

[AFTER - HUMANIZER 2.0]
Sarah presented the water sample report, but she had a migraine coming on and kept losing 
her place on slide four. Councilman Higgins was visibly checking his fantasy football score 
under the desk. When Sarah snapped at him to pay attention, she came across as shrill and 
defensive rather than heroic. Her colleague Karen kicked her ankle under the table to shut her up.
```

## Transformation Checklist: The 10-Point Scorecard

Before publishing or finalizing any narrative generation, grade it against this scorecard:

- [ ] **1. No Moralizing Wrap-Up**: Does the ending avoid explaining the lesson or theme?
- [ ] **2. No Somatic Reflex Clichés**: Are throat-tightening, chest-pounding, and cold sweats removed?
- [ ] **3. Explicit Emotions Named**: Does the text use clear, unpretentious emotional labels?
- [ ] **4. Olfactory Restraint**: Are smell metaphors used only when strictly necessary?
- [ ] **5. Asymmetric Subplot**: Is there at least one secondary subplot that doesn't mirror the main theme?
- [ ] **6. Nonlinear Timeline**: Are there flashbacks, time jumps, or asynchronous revelations?
- [ ] **7. External Resolution**: Is the climax influenced by external chance, accident, or third parties?
- [ ] **8. Moral Ambivalence**: Does the protagonist make at least one flawed or morally questionable choice?
- [ ] **9. Real-World Grounding**: Are there specific named books, places, or cultural anchors?
- [ ] **10. Voice & Dialogue Friction**: Does dialogue contain interruptions, petty stakes, and evasions?
