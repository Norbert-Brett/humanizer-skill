Elena sat on the radiator under the third-floor catwalk, checking the seals on the fuel manifold with an adjustable wrench. Three years ago, back at the drydock in Rotterdam, the inspector had warned her that these titanium bolts would strip after four pressurizations. He had been right about the third one.

Marcus came down the iron stairs with two cardboard cups of chicory coffee from the mess deck.

"Port Authority signed off on the ballast," he said, handing her a cup. "The cargo manifest still has the wrong customs stamp, though."

"Leave it," she said. "If the patrol cutter flags us at the perimeter, tell them the clerk in Antwerp misspelled the registration. They won't hold up thirty tons of hydroponic fertilizer over an invoice number."

She was exhausted, and more than a little angry that the transfer pumps had taken four hours instead of forty minutes. Outside, sunlight glared off the corrugated steel roofs of the cargo bays, bright and relentlessly hot.

Marcus took a sip and grimaced. "Coffee machine is scalding the grounds again."

"I know," Elena said. She picked up the torque wrench and went back to the flange.
